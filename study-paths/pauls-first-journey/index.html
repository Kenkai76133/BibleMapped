Checkpoint 01 · Place

Sent from Antioch

Read Acts 13:1–4

Antioch in Syria—not Jerusalem—became the launch point. This diverse church prayed, fasted, and commissioned Barnabas and Saul for the work.

Why the location matters

Antioch was a major eastern city of the Roman Empire and connected inland Syria to Mediterranean travel. The mission begins from a multicultural congregation positioned near major routes.

Checkpoint 02 · Route

Across Cyprus

Read Acts 13:4–7

From Seleucia they sailed to Salamis, then traveled across Cyprus toward Paphos. Barnabas was a native of Cyprus, making the island a meaningful first destination.

Trace the movement

Salamis stood on the eastern coast near Syria; Paphos was the Roman administrative center on the western side. Luke compresses an island-wide journey into a few sentences.

Checkpoint 03 · Person

Before the Roman governor

Read Acts 13:6–12

At Paphos, the missionaries encountered Sergius Paulus and Elymas. The passage also begins consistently calling Saul by his Roman name, Paul.

Why this is a turning point

The gospel is presented before a Roman provincial official, opposition is confronted, and Luke’s narrative increasingly centers Paul.

Checkpoint 04 · Message

Into southern Galatia

Read Acts 13:13–52

After landing at Perga, Paul’s group traveled inland and upward to Pisidian Antioch. Paul’s synagogue sermon retells Israel’s story and announces Jesus as the promised Savior.

Notice the geography

The journey from the coast into the Anatolian interior required a difficult climb through rugged country. Pisidian Antioch was distinct from the Syrian Antioch that sent them.

Checkpoint 05 · Culture

Misunderstood at Lystra

Read Acts 14:8–20

After a healing, the crowd identified Barnabas with Zeus and Paul with Hermes. The missionaries had to redirect worship toward the living Creator.

What the reaction reveals

The people interpreted the miracle through their own religious world. Paul appealed to creation and God’s provision as shared evidence.

Checkpoint 06 · Purpose

Strengthening the new churches

Read Acts 14:21–28

Paul and Barnabas returned through Lystra, Iconium, and Pisidian Antioch, appointing elders and encouraging the believers before sailing home.

Why they retraced the route

The mission was not merely about reaching new cities. They revisited vulnerable communities, provided local leadership, and reported to the church that sent them.
